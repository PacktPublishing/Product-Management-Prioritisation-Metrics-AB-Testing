# Capstone C.2: Build the Decision Pack
---

## What you are building

Next quarter at Tallyrow, Maya wants smart reminders now, a competitor has launched "AI reminders", three agencies want multi-currency, the e-invoicing mandate's next phase has a date, and Tomás wants late fees. You have **ten person-months**.

Your decision pack has five parts. Each one uses a skill you have already practised, and the scorecard at the end is how you (or a colleague) can check it.

| Part | What it answers | Skill you are using |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The ranked quarter | What do we commit to, and in what order? | Two prioritisation models, reconciled; MoSCoW for the timebox; dates scheduled first |
| 2. One idea, evaluated | Is the most contested idea worth building? | Assumption map and test card; Kano or product-market fit evidence |
| 3. The metric tree | How will we know the quarter worked? | North Star, inputs and guardrails with limits |
| 4. The experiment brief | How do we test the riskiest bet honestly? | Hypothesis, OEC, guardrails, unit, sample, window, decision rule |
| 5. The one-page memo | What does Maya need to read before the meeting? | Inputs, not scores; the trade-off in plain view |

---

## Part 1: The ranked quarter

1. **List every candidate** from the brief: the mandate's next phase, smart reminders (full build, or another test), multi-currency for agencies, late fees, the client portal, receipt capture, and anything the data pack shows that the brief does not name. *(Hint: read the funnel in the data pack carefully.)*
2. **Price the three lines of the bill** for the four most contested items: the slot (effort), the value (and how confident you are), and the weeks (cost of delay and urgency profile).
3. **Schedule date-bound work first.** For the mandate's next phase, write the deadline, the expected duration and the **last safe start week**. If it falls inside this quarter, it is in.
4. **Rank the rest with two models.** Pick one value model (RICE or weighted scoring) and one time model (CD3 or WSJF). Use the workbook tabs.
5. **Reconcile them.** Where the two rankings disagree, write one sentence on why: which line of the bill each model puts on top.
6. **Fit the timebox with MoSCoW.** Musts at or under about 60% of capacity, Coulds around 20%, Won't means "won't have this time".

## Part 2: One idea, evaluated

Choose the most contested idea (most people will pick smart reminders; multi-currency is an equally good choice).

1. **Map its assumptions** on importance × evidence: desirability, feasibility, viability, adaptability.
2. **Label every piece of evidence you have** as *planned* or *unplanned*, and note its weaknesses (volunteers? no control group? one segment found after the fact?).
3. **Write the test card** for the top-right assumption: hypothesis, test, metric, and the pass criterion, **before** imagining the result.

## Part 3: The metric tree

1. Start from Tallyrow's North Star, **invoices paid on time per week**, and its first level: sending businesses × invoices per business × on-time rate.
2. Go one level down on the branch your quarter is meant to move, and name the **input** each committed item should push.
3. Add **guardrails** with a baseline and a limit for each committed item, and the **agreed response** if one trips. Use the guardrail spec from measuring success for late fees if they are in your plan.

## Part 5: The one-page memo

Write it for Maya, who will read it on her phone before the meeting.

1. **The call** in three lines: Must, Should, Could, Won't (this time).
2. **The contested item** in four lines: its inputs, the line of the bill they price, the cheapest test that would change them, and when the answer arrives.
3. **What it costs** to do it her way instead: what drops out, and what that risks.
4. **How you'll know** it worked: the inputs and guardrails, and the date of the first readout.

## Scorecard: check your pack before the defence

| # | Check | ✓ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every date-bound item has a last safe start week, and the plan respects it | |
| 2 | Two models used; every disagreement explained by the line of the bill each puts on top | |
| 3 | Musts at or under about 60% of capacity; Won't items named "this time" | |
| 4 | Every confidence value has its evidence written next to it | |
| 5 | Unplanned evidence (for example a segment found after the fact) is treated as a hypothesis | |
| 6 | The test card's pass criterion was written before any result | |
| 7 | Every committed item has an input metric and a guardrail with a limit and an agreed response | |
| 8 | The experiment brief names its unit of randomisation and outcome window | |
| 9 | The memo leads with inputs, not scores, and states the cost of the alternative | |
| 10 | Nothing in the pack relies on a number you can't trace to the data pack | |

When every row has a tick, watch the defence and compare your pack with the worked answer.
